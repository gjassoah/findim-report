## O.2 Bounded dimension forces bounded behaviour

**Lemma O.2a.** Let D be finite-dimensional of finite global dimension, X a f.d. D-bimodule,
F = X ⊗ᴸ_D −, and d a dimension vector. Among N ∈ Rep_d(D) with finite extinction time
t(N) = min{t : F^t N ≃ 0}, the extinction times are bounded.

*Proof.* Since gldim D < ∞, X has a bounded resolution Q by bimodules that are f.g. projective as right
D-modules, and Q^{⊗_D t} is a bounded complex of such bimodules computing F^t. For N ∈ Rep_d(D), the complex
Q^{⊗t} ⊗_D N is a bounded complex of vector spaces whose differentials are given by matrices depending
polynomially on the point N; its term dimensions are constant for a fixed dimension vector (on a
total-dimension variety they are only locally constant, so one works on each of its finitely many
open-and-closed pieces — correction found by Codex). A bounded complex with fixed term dimensions v_i is
acyclic iff its ranks take prescribed values (determined recursively from one end), i.e. iff
rank d^i ≥ ρ_i for all i; this is an open condition. Hence E_t = {N : F^t N ≃ 0} is open in Rep_d(D), and
E_t ⊆ E_{t+1}. Rep_d(D) is a noetherian topological space, so the increasing chain of open sets E_t is
stationary, E_t = E_T for t ≥ T, and t(N) ≤ T whenever t(N) < ∞. ∎ (status: **AI-verified**; no prior
formulation located)

**Lemma O.2b.** Let A be finite-dimensional and d a dimension vector. Then {M ∈ Rep_d(A) : pd M ≤ n} is
open, and pd is bounded on the modules of finite projective dimension in Rep_d(A).

*Proof.* pd M ≤ n iff Tor^A_{n+1}(A/J, M) = 0 (J the radical). With a projective resolution Q of the right
module A/J, Tor_{n+1} is the homology of Q_{n+2} ⊗ M → Q_{n+1} ⊗ M → Q_n ⊗ M, a complex of fixed dimensions
with polynomially varying differentials; vanishing of its middle homology is a lower bound on the sum of two
ranks, an open condition. The second statement follows as in O.2a. ∎ (status: **AI-verified**; the same
argument is in Happel, *Homological conjectures in the representation theory of finite-dimensional
algebras*, §2.3, p. 5, attributed there to Schofield 1985; openness also follows from Geiß–Labardini-Fragoso–
Schröer, arXiv:2302.02085v2, Cor. 2.6 — both located by Codex)

*Consequences.* In any counterexample the witnesses have unbounded dimension. In the trivial-extension
mechanism, the modules N_m must have unbounded dimension. In the mechanism of O.4 below, the syzygies
Ω^n E must have unbounded dimension (the C_n = Tr Ω^{n−1}E have pd n and dimension bounded in terms of the
minimal presentation of Ω^{n−1}E). In particular, one-parameter families with Ω E_λ ≅ E_{qλ} and constant
dimension cannot produce the witnesses by themselves.

## O.3 Selection data need infinitely many independent rank functions

Generalised selection data: R a k-algebra, Ψ an R-bimodule that is f.g. projective as a right R-module,
H = Ψ ⊗_R − on f.d. left R-modules. (The preprint's case: Ψ = ₐ(eR) with e central, α an automorphism.)

**Lemma O.3.** For a f.d. left R-module Y put χ_Y(P) = dim_k (P ⊗_R Y) for f.g. projective right
R-modules P; this defines χ_Y : K₀(R_R) → ℤ. Let T : K₀(R_R) → K₀(R_R), [P] ↦ [P ⊗_R Ψ], and let V be the
image of span_ℚ{T^t [R] : t ≥ 0} in ℚ^{f.d. modules} under v ↦ (χ_Y(v))_Y. If dim V = r < ∞, then every
f.d. Y with H^t Y = 0 for some t satisfies H^r Y = 0.

*Proof.* χ_Y(T[P]) = χ_{HY}(P), so the common kernel of the χ_Y is T-stable and T acts on V; V is cyclic,
generated by v = [R]. Also dim H^t Y = χ_Y(T^t v) ≥ 0. Write V = V₀ ⊕ V₁ with T nilpotent on V₀ and
invertible on V₁, v = v₀ + v₁, and W = span{T^t v₁ : t ≥ 0}, on which T is invertible. If H^{t₀}Y = 0 then
H^t Y = 0 for t ≥ t₀, so χ_Y vanishes on T^t v for t ≥ max(t₀, r); for such t, T^t v = T^t v₁ ∈ W. These
vectors span T^{t}W = W, so χ_Y vanishes on W, and χ_Y(T^r v) = χ_Y(T^r v₁) = 0 (as T^r v₀ = 0). ∎ (status:
**AI-verified**)

*Consequences.* Unbounded extinction requires the classes [Ψ^{⊗t}] to have infinitely many linearly
independent rank functions on f.d. modules. This excludes, for instance, commutative noetherian R (ranks of
projectives are locally constant on finitely many components), path algebras of quivers and more generally
any R whose f.g. projectives are sums of finitely many indecomposables up to isomorphism, and every
universal localisation of a f.d. hereditary algebra (K₀(B) → K₀(B_Σ) is surjective: Schofield,
arXiv:0708.0257v1, Lemma 4.1, located by Codex). It explains why the preprint uses a group whose group algebra has infinitely many independent
central idempotents e₀e₁⋯e_{t−1}.

**Remark O.3'** (status: **plausible**, sketch only). Stages S2–S4 of the preprint should work verbatim for
generalised selection data with R finitely presented: write Ψ ≅ εR^n for an idempotent matrix ε, so that the
left action is a unital homomorphism ρ : R → εM_n(R)ε; in the Verdier quotient take U = (L^n, θ(ε)) with the
R-action θ∘ρ; the odd double lemma applies to any idempotent endomorphism. In particular e need not be
central, and α need only be a unital homomorphism R → eRe.

## O.4 Failure of the strong Nakayama conjecture gives infinite findim, with explicit witnesses

**Proposition O.4.** Let A be finite-dimensional and E ≠ 0 a f.g. right A-module with
Ext^i_{A^op}(E, A) = 0 for all i ≥ 0. Let P_• → E be a minimal projective resolution and, for n ≥ 1,
C_n = coker(P_{n−1}^* → P_n^*) = Tr Ω^{n−1}E, where (−)^* = Hom_{A^op}(−, A). Then pd_A C_n = n for all
n ≥ 1. In particular the left little finitistic dimension of A is infinite.

*Proof.* Applying (−)^* to P_• gives a complex 0 → P_0^* → P_1^* → P_2^* → ⋯ of f.g. projective left
modules whose cohomology is Ext^*(E, A) = 0; so it is exact, and there are exact sequences
0 → P_0^* → P_1^* → C_1 → 0 and 0 → C_n → P_{n+1}^* → C_{n+1} → 0 (n ≥ 1).
(i) C_1 is not projective: otherwise P_0^* → P_1^* is a split monomorphism, so its dual P_1 → P_0 is a
split epimorphism, which for a minimal resolution forces E = 0.
(ii) Hence pd C_1 = 1.
(iii) If pd C_n = n, the second sequence gives pd C_{n+1} ≤ n + 1, and for every module V and j ≥ 1 the long
exact Ext sequence with Ext^{≥1}(P_{n+1}^*, V) = 0 gives Ext^{j+1}(C_{n+1}, V) ≅ Ext^j(C_n, V). Choosing V
with Ext^n(C_n, V) ≠ 0 gives pd C_{n+1} = n + 1. ∎ (status: **AI-verified**. The mechanism is classical: Crawley-Boevey, *Noncommutative
Algebra 2* (Bielefeld notes, 2019/20), §3.2, Prop. 5 and its proof, p. 63, proves that finite findim of
A^op implies the strong Nakayama assertion by this dualisation; located by Codex. The exact formula
pd C_n = n is the dimension-shifting refinement.)

By duality Ext^i_{A^op}(E, A) ≅ D Tor^A_i(E, DA), so the hypothesis says that the derived Nakayama functor
kills E: E ⊗ᴸ_A DA ≃ 0. It forces pd E = ∞ (if pd E = n < ∞ then Ext^n(E, A) ≠ 0) and, by O.2b, syzygies of
unbounded dimension.

*Relation to the companion preprints.* The companion obtains a simple module S over Γ = End(A ⊕ M)^op with
Ext^i_Γ(S, Γ) = 0 for all i ≥ 0 (its Corollary 1.2(2)) and infinite **left** findim via dominant dimension;
O.4 applied to this left module (A = Γ^op) gives infinite **right** findim of Γ from the first property alone.

*What O.4 changes for the search.* It gives a target condition that is checkable degree by degree by
computer over an effective field (a finite run of zero Ext groups certifies nothing; vanishing in all
degrees needs a proof) (minimal resolution of a candidate E and dimensions of Ext^i(E, A)), whose witnesses are explicit,
and whose proof is elementary once Ext^*(E, A) = 0 is proved, for example from a recursive description of
the resolution. The strong Nakayama conjecture holds for A^op whenever left findim A < ∞ (by O.4), so this
route is no easier in principle than the original problem, but it may be easier to search and to verify.

## O.5 From an Auslander–Reiten counterexample to infinite findim, with one more simple module

**Proposition O.5.** Let Λ be finite-dimensional and M a f.g. nonprojective left Λ-module with
Ext^i_Λ(M, M ⊕ Λ) = 0 for all i ≥ 1. Put G = Λ ⊕ M, Γ = End_Λ(G)^op and F = Hom_Λ(G, −), so that F(X) is
a left Γ-module and F restricts to an equivalence add G ≃ proj Γ with F(G) = Γ. Let π : P → M be a
projective cover and S = coker(F π). Then S ≠ 0 and Ext^i_Γ(S, Γ) = 0 for all i ≥ 0. Consequently (O.4
applied to Γ^op) the algebra End_Λ(Λ ⊕ M) has infinite left little finitistic dimension, with witnesses
Tr Ω^{n−1} S (transposes over Γ), and its number of simple modules is the number of isomorphism classes of
indecomposable summands of Λ ⊕ M.

*Proof.* (a) Resolution. Put K₀ = ker π and choose inductively right add G-approximations g_j : G_j → K_j
with K_{j+1} = ker g_j; they are surjective because Λ ∈ add G. This gives an exact sequence
⋯ → G₁ → G₀ → P → M → 0 with all terms in add G. Applying F: F is left exact, F(K_j) = ker F(g_{j−1})
(resp. ker Fπ for j = 0) and F(G_j) → F(K_j) is surjective by the approximation property. Hence
⋯ → F G₁ → F G₀ → F P → F M → S → 0 is a projective resolution of S.
(b) S ≠ 0: if Fπ were surjective, id_M (on the summand M of G) would lift through π, so π would split and
M would be projective.
(c) Ext into Γ. For Y ∈ add G, Hom_Γ(FY, Γ) = Hom_Γ(FY, FG) ≅ Hom_Λ(Y, G) naturally (Yoneda on add G). So
Ext^i_Γ(S, Γ) is the cohomology in degree i of 0 → Hom_Λ(M, G) → Hom_Λ(P, G) → Hom_Λ(G₀, G) → ⋯ (with
Hom_Λ(M, G) in degree 0).
(d) This complex is exact. The modules P, G_j lie in add G and Ext^{≥1}_Λ(G, G) = 0 (the hypothesis, since
Ext^{≥1}(Λ, −) = 0); so the resolution ⋯ → G₀ → P → M computes Ext^*_Λ(M, G) after applying Hom(−, G). By
hypothesis Ext^{≥1}_Λ(M, G) = 0, so the complex Hom(P, G) → Hom(G₀, G) → ⋯ has cohomology Hom_Λ(M, G) in
degree 0 and none elsewhere; including the term Hom_Λ(M, G) (which maps injectively, π being surjective)
gives an exact complex. ∎ (status: **AI-verified**, Codex job 03, `audit/03-verification-O5-codex.md`, with two
explicit tests showing which Ext groups survive when the hypotheses fail. Classical source: Auslander–Reiten,
*On a generalized version of the Nakayama conjecture*, Proc. AMS 52 (1975), Theorem 1.1(b), p. 71, proof
p. 72 — located and read by Codex in the Library copy AR75a; their proof uses injective resolutions)

*Application* (status: **conditional** on the explicit counterexample to the Auslander–Reiten conjecture,
OpenAI, 2026-09-23, Theorem 1.1, which is unverified here). There Λ has eight simple modules and
Z = (X, Y, ι) is built from the one-dimensional simple X = s ⊗ s. Z need not be indecomposable (the
construction allows projective summands), but any indecomposable nonprojective summand Z′ of Z inherits
Ext^{≥1}(Z′, Z′ ⊕ Λ) = 0, so End_Λ(Λ ⊕ Z′) is an algebra over 𝔽₂(q, H₁, H₂) with **nine** simple modules and
infinite little finitistic dimension. It avoids the symmetric-algebra transfer of the Tachikawa companion.
**Size:** with the finite representatives exactly as specified in the AR preprint, Codex computed
dim F = 800 + 159999⁵·dim C₁ and dim Λ = 1600 + 159999⁵·dim C₁ with dim C₁ = 1 623 889 344, i.e.
dim Λ ≈ 1.7·10³⁵ (status: AI-proved from the preprint's definitions; `audit/03-verification-O5-codex.md`,
§5). The cosyzygies over A^e (dim 160000) used to build F cause this. A minimal representative of F (up to
projective summands) might be far smaller; this is unexamined. As specified, the candidate is not tractable
by hand or by computer.

## Summary of the necessary conditions found so far

Any counterexample A (with witnesses N_m or C_n):

1. has witnesses of unbounded dimension (O.2);
2. lies outside the known positive classes (monomial, rad³ = 0, representation dimension ≤ 3, Gorenstein,
   …; to be read in the sources with exact hypotheses);
3. if it arises from selection data (R, Ψ), needs infinitely many independent rank functions [Ψ^{⊗t}] (O.3);
4. if it is a trivial extension D ⋉ X with gldim D < ∞, needs F-iterates of zero class in K₀(D) after rank
   K₀(D) steps (note 01, §3).
